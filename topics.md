<section class="section editorial">
  <div class="shell editorial__list">
    <article id="collective-intelligence" class="editorial__entry">
      <span class="editorial__number">01</span>
      <div>
        <p class="section__eyebrow">Emergence · coordination · adaptation</p>
        <h2>Collective Intelligence<br>&amp; Emergent Complexity</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="editorial__copy">
        <p>How can a group know more than any of its members? I am interested in the local interactions that produce global order—from animal swarms and markets to scientific communities and online publics.</p>
        <p class="editorial__question">Current question: when does coordination become intelligence, and who gets to decide?</p>
      </div>
    </article>

    <article id="cybernetics" class="editorial__entry">
      <span class="editorial__number">02</span>
      <div>
        <p class="section__eyebrow">Feedback · control · design</p>
        <h2>Cybernetics,<br>Feedback Systems &amp; Design</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="editorial__copy">
        <p>Cybernetics offers a language for systems that sense, compare, and act. I use that language critically: to understand not only how feedback enables learning, but how measurements and goals reshape the worlds they claim to describe.</p>
        <p class="editorial__question">Current question: how do we design feedback without mistaking the signal for the system?</p>
      </div>
    </article>

    <article id="histories" class="editorial__entry">
      <span class="editorial__number">03</span>
      <div>
        <p class="section__eyebrow">Archives · infrastructure · power</p>
        <h2>Cultural, Computational<br>&amp; Economic History</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="editorial__copy">
        <p>Technical systems arrive with histories inside them. Tracing those histories—of classification, calculation, labor, and exchange—helps reveal which assumptions have hardened into infrastructure.</p>
        <p class="editorial__question">Current question: what old institutions are speaking through our newest machines?</p>
      </div>
    </article>

    <article id="machine-learning" class="editorial__entry">
      <span class="editorial__number">04</span>
      <div>
        <p class="section__eyebrow">Data · models · artificial worlds</p>
        <h2>Big Data, Reinforcement<br>&amp; Machine Learning</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="editorial__copy">
        <p>Machine learning is both a research method and an object of study. I am interested in models that learn through interaction, the social worlds represented by datasets, and tools that remain legible to the communities using them.</p>
        <p class="editorial__question">Current question: what changes when a model becomes a participant rather than an observer?</p>
      </div>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>
