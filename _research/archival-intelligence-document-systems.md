Archives are not merely collections of pages. They are sequences, annotations, institutional practices, and contested descriptions assembled over time. This program studies how computational systems can work with those structures without flattening them.

The projects combine page layout, named entities, language models, structured-output parsing, and historical interpretation. The recurring challenge is to make records usable for computation while keeping the provenance and uncertainty needed for responsible scholarship.

## Guiding questions

- Which document signals remain reliable across heterogeneous archival collections?
- How should extraction pipelines represent uncertainty, sequence, and missing information?
- When does a model improve access, and when does it introduce a new layer of opacity?
- How can historians inspect, correct, and reuse machine-generated structures?

The related projects below address different parts of that program: building datasets, detecting document boundaries, transforming PDFs into relational structures, and evaluating historical-language translation.
