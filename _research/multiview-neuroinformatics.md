High-dimensional EEG biomarkers and phenotype measures describe the same participants from different, incomplete perspectives. This research program examines how those views can be reduced, aligned, and tested without confusing visual separation or in-sample fit with reproducible structure.

The work treats validation as part of the representation itself. Preprocessing is fitted within training folds, developmental and demographic effects are handled explicitly, and null findings are reported when an apparently expressive representation does not support the proposed clinical interpretation.

## Guiding questions

- Does a representation preserve shared structure across EEG and phenotype views?
- Is it stable across folds, perturbations, and participant samples?
- Does a graph or embedding add interpretable evidence beyond a simpler baseline?
- Can developmental signal be separated from symptom-associated structure?

The four related case studies explore these questions through developmental deviation, stable feature supports, latent-structure benchmarking, and connectivity-preserving dimension reduction.
